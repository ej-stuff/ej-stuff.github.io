Why, probably

Karakin is the tiny 2×2km map with almost no vehicles worth using and constant close-range pressure — the fact you land top-10 there more than any other map says your survival instincts are genuinely good in the chaos. The fact you've never won it says the opposite habit is costing you: Karakin's final circles are small enough that turtling rarely wins, so the fix is probably fighting more, not less, once you're inside the top 4–5.

Sanhok is the opposite problem. It's dense, fast, and the fights start early and close — it punishes a patient, mid-range playstyle, which is exactly your playstyle (your best weapons, SLR/Mini14/Mk12 SPR/AK-M, all want space to work). If Sanhok comes up, expect a fight inside the first two minutes and loot with that in mind rather than trying to out-rotate the map.

Deston (3 games) and Rondo (4 games) don't have enough data to draw real conclusions from — though "zero kills in four straight games on Rondo" is at least a fun thing to notice.

Landing isn't the problem

Only 11 of 143 games (7.7%) end inside the first 90 seconds — most drop-and-die panic that sinks casual squads isn't really your issue. Whatever trouble finds you, it mostly finds you later, once the real game has started.

You go down together — a lot. In exactly half of your non-win games, both of you were eliminated in the same recorded second. That's not a coincidence at that rate — when a fight turns against you, it turns fast for both of you at once, which usually means you're standing close enough that one grenade or one lucky spray can end the game twice over. Simple fix: when you're holding an angle together, put a few real meters (and ideally a wall) between you. If it goes wrong, make the enemy have to work for both kills separately.

For what it's worth, the weapon that "kills" you most isn't a person at all — blue-zone/bleed-out damage accounts for more eliminations (8) than any single enemy in 143 games of strangers. The circle doesn't miss, and it doesn't care how good your aim is.

08

Coach's Notes

Five things the data actually supports — not generic PUBG advice, things that show up specifically in your 143 games.

  1. Spread out when a fight is live

    Half your losses end with both of you eliminated in the same second. A few meters of separation and a piece of cover between you is free insurance against one grenade or one lucky burst ending the whole game at once.

  2. Let fights come to your sightline, not the other way around

    Every one of your best accuracy numbers — SLR, Mini 14, AK-M, Mk12 SPR — is a single-fire gun used at range. Pushing into buildings for close-quarters fights plays against your actual strengths; holding an angle and letting the enemy walk into it plays to them.

  3. On Karakin, finish the job

    Best top-10 rate of any map (61.5%) but zero wins. You already out-survive almost everyone there — the final circles on a 2×2km map are too small to hide in, so treat top-4–5 there as the moment to fight, not the moment to turtle.

  4. On Sanhok, front-load the aggression

    It's the one map actively working against your patient, mid-range style. Loot fast, expect contact inside the first two minutes, and don't be surprised when the DMRs don't get room to breathe — that's the map, not you.

  5. Keep stacking sessions, not hours

    Your entire improvement arc — 31.9 to 17.5 average placement, 4% to 48% top-10 rate — happened across about a dozen play days in five weeks, with zero measurable fatigue drop-off even in 21-game marathons. You don't need more time to keep improving. You're clearly learning fast with the time you do get.
