What the math says, updated

With roughly 46 duo teams in an average lobby, landing in the top 10 by pure chance happens about 1 in 4.6 tries — 22%. Your first 25 games hit it once (4%), which is worse than random and completely normal while everything is new. Your most recent 25 hit it five times (20%) at an average finish of 19.4 — right around the random line on top-10s, but still 12 places better on average than where you started.

The best 25-game window in the dataset ended on 12 September: 14.6 average, 15 of 25 in the top 10 (60%). That's the run the last version of this page was written in. Since then the rolling line has gone up and down. It dipped to 13.4 around 20 September, rose to 22.9 in the 22 September session (the same night as the third chicken dinner), and ends at 18.1. The best 10-game stretch ever is still the 9.5 around game 123 (6 July).

A trailing-25 window swings hard on one session, so don't over-read the run or the cool-off. The durable claim the whole chart supports: you improved a lot early, then settled into a band of roughly 15–25 average with hot and cold stretches, and your good weeks are better than they used to be.

Why, probably

Karakin is the proven case: best average and a 57.6% top-10 rate on 33 games, and still zero wins. You out-survive the chaos on this tiny map better than anywhere else, but Karakin's final circles are too small to hide in, so the fix is fighting more once you're inside the top 4–5, not less. Deston looks even better on the top-10 rate but has only 7 duo games.

Sanhok (28.4 avg, 16.7% top-10) is now clearly the problem map. With the solo and arcade games taken out, it has the worst average, and the worst top-10 rate of any map with more than 8 games. It's dense and close-range, which fights your patient, mid-range weapon picks. Taego (24.9 avg, 19.4% top-10) is the other weak one, though it's also where the third win came from.

Rondo (19.8 avg, 10 games) and Paramo (17.9 avg, 8 games) sit in the upper half on average, but Paramo's top-10 rate is only 12.5% (1 of 8). Small samples both ways.

Death Habits

Landing isn't the problem

Only 9 games in 285 ended inside 90 seconds. Whatever trouble finds you, it mostly finds you later, once the real game has started. When you do go out without a win, you usually go out together: in 55% of those games you both died at the same moment, and in every one of them at least one of you had been knocked first.

The recoverable mistake is still the un-revived knock. Of 413 knockdowns, 120 ended with a pickup — 29%. When a revive does happen it's fast (26.1 seconds on average, against the roughly two minutes PUBG gives you), so this was never a reaction-speed problem. It's about whether the knocked player can be reached at all.

The Engagement Report looks at where revives go wrong: almost one in three revives during a fight ends with one of you knocked again within 15 seconds, smoke or no smoke. Get behind hard cover first.

Coach's Notes

Six things the data actually supports — refreshed for 285 duo games.

  1. The early-September run was real, and so is the cool-off

    The best 25-game stretch ever ended on 12 Sep (14.6 average, 60% top-10). The 25 games since average 19.4 with a 20% top-10 rate. Both fit the same picture: a band of about 15–25 with hot and cold weeks. Look at what the good sessions had in common before changing anything.

  2. Convert more knocks into revives

    29% of your 413 knockdowns end in a pickup. That's still a 71% failure rate on the single most recoverable mistake in the data. When it goes wrong, break line of sight first and reach the body — or disengage as a pair rather than holding the position solo.

  3. The M24 deserves more time in the loadout

    50.9% accuracy across 110 shots, 16 kills — more than double the accuracy of anything else either of you carries. SLR (18.1%), Dragunov (18.8%) and Mini 14 (15.6%) round out a strong precision tier, but the M24 specifically is worth prioritizing when it's on the ground.

  4. On Karakin, finish the job

    Best average (11.1) and top-10 rate (57.6%) on 33 games, still zero wins. The final circles are too small to hide in, so treat top-4–5 there as the moment to fight, not the moment to turtle. Deston looks just as good but on only 7 games.

  5. Sanhok is the map to figure out

    Worst average (28.4) and worst top-10 rate on a real sample (16.7%, 24 games). It's dense and close-range, the opposite of the long-gun openers that win you fights elsewhere. Worth a dedicated look: where you land, and whether you're fighting at the ranges you're good at.

  6. You're sharper at the end of a session than the start

    Across every 6+ game session combined, the last third averages a 19.2 placement against 24.2 for the first third. Treat game one of a sitting as a warm-up.
