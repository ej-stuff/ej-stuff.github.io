What the math says, updated

With roughly 46 duo teams in an average lobby, landing in the top 10 by pure chance happens about 1 in 4.6 tries — 22%. Your first 25 games hit it once (4%), which is worse than random and completely normal while everything is new. Your most recent 25 hit it five times (20%), at an average finish of 23.7.

Read honestly, that is a real improvement on where you started but not the steep climb the June version of this page suggested. At the 213-match mark the trailing 25 games averaged 16.9 with a 44% top-10 rate; the 16 games added on 30 August were a mixed bag and pulled that trailing window back down. The 10-game rolling line finishes around 22 — much better than the 33 you opened with, and roughly flat for the last hundred games.

A trailing-25 window swings hard on one session, so neither the June spike nor this dip should be over-read. The durable claim is the one the whole chart supports: you improved a lot early and have been steady since.

Landing isn't the problem

Only 9 of 229 games (3.9%) end inside the first 90 seconds, and that has improved since the last read. Whatever trouble finds you, it mostly finds you later, once the real game has started.

The recoverable mistake is the un-revived knock. Across 306 knockdowns, only 80 ended with a pickup. When a revive does happen it is fast — 25.4 seconds on average, against the roughly two minutes PUBG gives you — so this isn't a reaction-speed problem. It's that in three-quarters of cases the pickup is never attempted or never reachable, and a teammate who stays down is a teammate you are about to lose.

The Engagement Report digs into exactly when those pickups succeed and fail — the short version is that revive rate collapses once you're more than about 25 metres apart when the knock lands.

08

Coach's Notes

Five things the data actually supports — refreshed for 229 games.

  1. Convert more knocks into revives

    Only 26% of your 306 knockdowns end in a pickup, and a teammate left down is a teammate about to be lost. When it goes wrong, break line of sight first and reach the body — or disengage as a pair rather than holding the position solo.

  2. Let fights come to your sightline, not the other way around

    SLR (17.0%), Mini 14 (17.4%), SKS (20.0%), Dragunov (24.3%) — your best accuracy numbers are all single-fire guns used at range, and that pattern only got stronger with more shots on record. Holding an angle and letting the enemy walk into it plays to your actual strengths; pushing into buildings plays against them.

  3. On Karakin, finish the job

    Best top-10 rate of any map (60%, now on 25 games) but still zero wins. The final circles on a 2×2km map are too small to hide in — treat top-4–5 there as the moment to fight, not the moment to turtle.

  4. Taego has quietly become your problem map, not just Sanhok

    Sanhok is still the worst by average placement, but Taego now has the worst top-10 rate (17.4%) on a real sample (23 games). Different map, different shape of fight — worth paying attention to specifically next time it comes up, since the "why" isn't obvious yet.

  5. You're sharper at the end of a session than the start

    Across every 6+ game session combined, the last third averages a 21.0 placement against 25.7 for the first third. That's the opposite of fatigue — treat the first game or two of a sitting as a warm-up, not a data point.
