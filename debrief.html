What the math says, updated

With roughly 46 duo teams in an average lobby, landing in the top 10 by pure chance happens about 1 in 4.6 tries — 22%. Your first 25 games hit it once (4%), which is worse than random and completely normal while everything is new. Your most recent 25 hit it fourteen times — 56%, at an average finish of 15.8. That is not a typo: this is the best 25-game window in the whole dataset, comfortably ahead of the 44% top-10 rate the 213-match read found back in July.

The last version of this page flagged a dip — a mixed session on 30 August had pulled the trailing window back down to 23.7 avg / 20% top-10. That dip is fully gone, and it kept moving after: the rolling line climbed to 25.9 around game 240 (one more rough patch), then fell hard to 11.4 at game 250 — the second-best 10-game stretch in the whole dataset, behind only a 9.5 spike back around game 127 — before settling into the high teens (17.8) for the last handful of games. Sessions on 5 and 11 September carried most of it.

A trailing-25 window swings hard on one session, so don't over-read either the July dip or this run. The durable claim the whole chart supports: you improved a lot early, went flat for a long middle stretch, and are now trending up again for the first time since June.

Signature Weapons

Same duo, mostly the same guns — but this read found a gun that was invisible on every previous version of this page, and it isn't a small one.

S821's real second weapon just appeared out of nowhere: the RPD. 1,730 shots and 17 kills, more used than the M249 or HK416 — and it was completely missing from every earlier cut of this data. The cause was a naming-table bug: PUBG logs a fired shot under one internal ID (Item_Weapon_RPD_C) and a kill under a different one (WeapRPD_C), and the RPD was the one weapon in the whole armory missing the mapping between the two — so every RPD shot, hit, and kill silently failed to join up and got dropped from every weapon table on the site, including this page and Weapon Stats. Fixed as part of this refresh (RPD, plus the same gap for Groza, Vector and MG3). Nothing changed in how you played — only in whether the site could see it.

The single most striking number in this whole dataset: S821's M24 sits at 52.2% accuracy across 90 shots — 12 kills, 3,109 damage. That's a genuinely large sample for a bolt-action, and more than double any other gun in the armory. The rest of the precision tier is still strong on its own terms — SLR 18.5%, Mini 14 17.7%, Dragunov 19.1% on 110 shots — but the M24 is in a different category entirely. Casual full-auto accuracy in PUBG typically runs single digits to low teens; 52% on a sniper rifle is basically never missing a shot you actually take.

Landing isn't the problem

The count of quick wipes (games ending inside 90 seconds) hasn't moved at all — still exactly 9, even with 39 more games added. Every one of those new games survived the opening minute and a half. Whatever trouble finds you, it still mostly finds you later, once the real game has started.

The recoverable mistake is still the un-revived knock — but it's recovering more often. Across 372 knockdowns (up from 306), 110 ended with a pickup — a 30% conversion rate, up from 26% at the last read. When a revive does happen it is still fast — 26.3 seconds on average, against the roughly two minutes PUBG gives you — so this was never a reaction-speed problem. It's still true that most knocks (70%) end with no pickup, but the trend on this specific number is now pointing the right way instead of sitting flat.

The Engagement Report digs into exactly when those pickups succeed and fail — the short version is that revive rate collapses once you're more than about 25 metres apart when the knock lands.

Coach's Notes

Six things the data actually supports — refreshed for 268 games.

  1. Your recent form is the real story — don't let one flat summer bury it

    Last 25 games: 15.8 average placement, 56% top-10 rate. That's not a fluke reading on a noisy window — it's the best 25-game stretch in the dataset by a wide margin. Whatever changed in September, keep doing it before looking for anything else to fix.

  2. Convert more knocks into revives — and keep pushing the number that's already moving

    30% of your 372 knockdowns now end in a pickup, up from 26% last read. Progress, but still a 70% failure rate on the single most recoverable mistake in the data. When it goes wrong, break line of sight first and reach the body — or disengage as a pair rather than holding the position solo.

  3. The M24 deserves more time in the loadout

    52.2% accuracy across 90 shots is not a small-sample fluke anymore — it's more than double the accuracy of anything else in the armory, on a real number of shots. SLR (18.5%), Mini 14 (17.7%) and Dragunov (19.1%) round out a genuinely strong precision tier, but the M24 specifically is worth prioritizing when it's on the ground.

  4. On Karakin, finish the job — and take a first look at Deston

    Karakin: best-proven top-10 rate (56.7%, 30 games) but still zero wins — the final circles are too small to hide in, so treat top-4–5 there as the moment to fight, not the moment to turtle. Deston has posted an even better line (77.8% top-10, 10.9 avg) but on only 9 games — worth tracking, not yet worth rebuilding a strategy around.

  5. Taego is still the map to figure out

    Worst top-10 rate on the board (17.4%) on a real sample (23 games), and that number hasn't budged since the last read despite 39 more games played elsewhere. Sanhok's problem (dense, close-range) is well understood; Taego's isn't yet — a big vehicle-heavy map converting decent average placements into so few actual top-10s is worth a dedicated look next time it comes up.

  6. You're sharper at the end of a session than the start — and the warm-up is shrinking

    Across every 6+ game session combined, the last third averages a 20.7 placement against 25.1 for the first third. Last read had that same last-third number (20.7) paired with a worse first-third (26.4) — so the back half of a session hasn't gotten any better, you're just getting there faster. Treat game one of a sitting as a warm-up, but expect less of a penalty for it than before.
