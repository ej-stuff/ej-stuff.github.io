The Engagement Report

Every fight from 287 matches rebuilt from telemetry and replayed shot by shot — who fired, at whom, from how far, and how many seconds passed before the second of you joined in. Fewer averages, more actual fights, with the first five minutes of every match excluded so drop-zone chaos doesn't dilute the numbers.

D282 & S821 · Duo FPP · 2 Jun – 20 Sep 2026

This report is built around the fights themselves rather than around averages. Every combat event is grouped into a discrete engagement, reconstructed second by second, and sorted into the situation it belongs to. You can read any individual fight in Chapter 6, or explore the whole dataset live — filterable by game stage, map, weapon and more, with charts that update as you filter — on the Engagement Stats page.

Every chapter below except Chapter 6's browser table excludes the first five minutes of each match — drop-zone chaos, 3rd-partying, half-found loadouts, random weapons — before computing anything. 155 of 516 decisive fights fall in that window. Chapter 6 and the Engagement Stats page show everything, filterable either way.

Health is measured from the last position ping strictly before first contact — the closest available read on what each player's HP actually was walking into the fight, rather than a nearby ping that might already include damage taken once the fight was under way.

In 67.8% of ambushes, neither of you had fired a shot. 141 of the 208 fights where the enemy hit first began with both of you completely unaware — no shots fired, no contact made. Those win 7.8%. This isn't a gunfight you're losing; it's a gunfight you didn't know had started until you were already down 40 health.

Worth being precise: PUBG doesn't log misses, so this measures who connects first, not who pulls the trigger first. Whether you fired before being hit turns out not to predict anything at all — it's landing the first hit that matters, which is a question of already having your sights on the right place.

It isn't just "we died before he could shoot"

That's the obvious objection so it's worth killing off: restricting to fights that lasted more than 30 seconds — plenty of time for both of you to join — fighting together still wins 22.2% (n=99) against 9.5% (n=84) alone. The effect survives the control.

Timing matters more than participation. When the second gun arrives inside 2 seconds you win 45.8% — comfortably the best category here. Between 2 and 15 seconds it drops to 12–24%, and even joining late (>15s) still beats not joining at all (29.0%). The pattern holds: both of you opening essentially at once is what "both ready before we engage" actually buys you, but showing up at all clearly beats fighting solo at any lag.

Sometimes nothing measurable separates them. The knife-range pair is the starkest example here — 16.5m vs 16.9m, both sides at full health, neither player's teammate involved yet, distance to within half a metre — and one side deals more than it takes (75-to-68) while the other is run over (30-to-100). The caught-in-a-vehicle pair is the one exception with a visible tell: the winner went in at 78 health against the loser's 91, so being hurt going in may have sharpened reactions here rather than hurt them — or it's just three data points and noise. Whatever decided the knife-range pair (who saw whom, who was already aiming at the door, who hit their shots) isn't in the telemetry.

That's the honest ceiling on this kind of analysis, and it's why the useful findings are the big structural ones — which fight you take — rather than fine-grained tuning. You can't out-analyse a coin flip at 10 metres; you can avoid being at 10 metres without the first shot.

Did The Advice Land?

Making sure you're both ready before engaging is a specific, dated change — it started around 26 August. Splitting the data at that line shows a real effect, but a more textured one than a single headline number would suggest.

Six sessions in — mostly holding, not uniformly

30 Aug (17 fights, 29.4% win rate), 31 Aug (12, 8.3%), 5 Sep (28, 25.0%), 11 Sep (24, 25.0%), 12 Sep (21, 33.3%) — four of those five clear the 16.5% pre-advice baseline comfortably. The exception is 31 Aug: a genuine dip to 8.3%, not just noise around baseline, though on a small sample. Two more sessions since (13 Sep, 20 Sep) are single-digit fight counts (n=1, n=3) and aren't worth reading anything into yet. Taken together, the 106 post-advice fights still average well clear of baseline — but it's no longer accurate to say every session has landed above it.

The mechanism is first contact, not shared damage. The "both fired" number is down (29.2%, from 39.6%), so this isn't about dealing more damage together. What's up is how often you land the first hit (45.3% vs 41.2% baseline). Being ready together looks like it makes you spot people first, rather than simply adding a second gun to the fight.

Still worth a note of caution on "full HP at contact" (56.5%→53.8%, down) and "started in vehicle" (14.1%→13.2%, down) — neither is the mechanism, and both moved slightly the "wrong" way without hurting the win rate, which is a useful reminder that not every number needs to move for the important one to.

What To Focus On

Short list, because only a short list survived. Everything here is either the dominant effect or a specific situation with a specific answer.

Dominant · n=361

1. Win the first hit, or don't take the fight

You hit first: 33.3%. They hit first: 8.2%. In 67.8% of ambushes neither of you had fired at all, and it still takes 6.5 seconds to shoot back. Every situation in the table is downstream of this one number — it holds up whether or not the first five minutes of the match are counted.

Do: pre-aim the angle you're about to clear at head height so first contact needs a small correction, not a sweep. And treat "taking fire from somewhere unknown" as an automatic break-contact call — the 8.2% is what happens when you try to fight it out instead.

Second largest · n=132 vs 229 · six sessions since the advice, one a dip

2. Keep doing whatever you started on 30 August

Both firing wins 28.8% vs 13.1%, and it holds when you restrict to long fights (22.2% vs 9.5%). Second gun inside 2 seconds still wins the most of any bucket on the page (45.8%). Since the advice landed, first-hit rate is up to 45.3% and win rate to 24.5% — real, across 106 fights and six sessions, though one of those sessions (31 Aug) dipped below the old baseline, so this isn't a one-way ratchet.

Do: keep the "both set before we open" habit — the data still says its real payoff is spotting them first, not the second gun. Call the target, confirm you both have the angle, then open together.

Specific · n=15, zero wins

3. Indoors and taking fire = leave, not fight

Ambushed inside a building: 0 wins in 15 attempts, average damage deficit 150.

Do: if the first shot comes while you're in a building you didn't clear, get out of the doorway sightline immediately rather than trading. There is still no example in 287 matches of that fight going your way.

Specific · n=28

4. Dismount before the last stretch

Being ambushed while mounted wins 10.7% with a −110 damage deficit. (Driving in general is fine — it's specifically being caught while stopped or slow-moving with an enemy already shooting that hurts.)

Do: stop 200–300m short of where you're actually going and walk in. You give up nothing but the noise that told everyone you were coming.

Moderate · n=51

5. Take the high side when it's free

More than 5m above the enemy: 39.2% win rate, against 15.7% on level ground. Being at a height disadvantage (14.9%) turns out to be barely worse than level ground once drop-phase fights are excluded — the real edge here is entirely about having the high ground yourself, not about avoiding low ground. You get the advantageous position in about 14% of fights.

Do: when two positions both cover the angle, take the higher one. Not worth a detour; worth the choice.

If you only remember one line: your fights are decided before the shooting starts. 148 of 361 begin with them ambushing you and those win 0–16%; 83 begin with a clean opener and those win 34–59%. Everything worth practising is about which of those two you're in.

Method & Caveats

  • Source: raw PUBG telemetry, 287 matches, 2 Jun – 20 Sep 2026. Position pings every 10s per player plus every damage, knock, kill and shot event.
  • A fight = combat events involving either player, split whenever 45s pass with no combat. 767 total; 21 bot-only fights excluded; 230 ended with nobody downed and are excluded from win rates. 516 decisive fights remain.
  • Early game = fight starts within 5 minutes of the match beginning (roughly the drop/loot window — 3rd-partying, half-found loadouts, everyone landing in the same three buildings). 155 of the 516 decisive fights fall in that window and are excluded from every chapter above except Chapter 6's browser table and the Engagement Stats page, both of which show everything and let you filter either way. 361 fights remain after the cut (68 wins, 104 trades, 189 losses; baseline win rate 18.8%).
  • Health is the last position ping strictly before first contact — the state each player was in walking into the fight, not contaminated by damage taken once it started.
  • "Hit first" = landed the first damaging hit. Misses aren't logged, so this is who connects, not who fires.
  • Situations are assigned by priority, most-decisive condition first, so each fight appears in exactly one row.
  • Small samples are shown inline. Anything under about n=20 (fighting a man down, n=9) is indicative, not settled. The post-advice comparison in Chapter 5 (n=106 across six sessions) is past that threshold, though its weakest session (31 Aug) is not on its own.
  • These are correlations from your own games, not experiments. The "both fired" result is the only one I could control properly; it held.
