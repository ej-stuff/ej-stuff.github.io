The Engagement Report

Every fight from 213 matches rebuilt from raw telemetry — positions, elevation, health, spacing, who moved toward whom, who fired first, and how long before the second player joined in. Then: what separates the fights you win from the ones you lose.

D282 & S821 · Duo FPP · 2 Jun – 25 Aug 2026

Every combat event was pulled from the raw match telemetry and grouped into discrete fights (a new fight starts after 45 seconds of silence). For each one I reconstructed the situation from position pings taken every 10 seconds: where both of you were, how far apart, how fast you were moving, whether you were in a vehicle, your health, your elevation relative to the enemy, whether they were closing on you or you on them, who landed the first hit, and how many seconds passed before the second of you started shooting.

That gives 365 decisive fights with about thirty measurements each. The interesting part isn't any single number — it's that when you line the wins up against the losses, four things separate them and the rest barely move at all.

01

Wins vs Losses, Feature by Feature

63 clean wins against 193 clean losses. Every measurement, side by side. Most of them are nearly identical — which is exactly what makes the four that aren't worth acting on.

Share of fights where each condition was true, in wins vs losses. Bars far apart = the thing actually matters. Bars level = it doesn't.

The four that separate wins from losses: you landed the first hit (76% of wins vs 21% of losses), both of you actually shot at the enemy (57% vs 18%), you went in at full health (65% vs 33%), and you had the high ground (18% vs 7%). Everything else — circle position, spacing, whether you were moving or holding, terrain, how many enemies — is close to a coin flip.

The measurements that didn't matter

Worth stating plainly, because it saves you effort: median distance from the circle centre was 0.41 in both wins and losses. Duo spacing at contact was 25m vs 23m. Fight duration 23s vs 22s. Number of enemies, 2 vs 2. Whether you were standing still or moving, near-identical. Even "did we fire a shot before getting hit" is flat (43% vs 45%) — shooting first isn't the same as hitting first, and only hitting counts.

The one mild surprise: in fights you won, the enemy had closed 38m on you in the previous 30 seconds; in fights you lost, only 20m. Letting them walk to you is better than walking to them. More on that in Chapter 05.

In two-thirds of the fights where you're hit first, you hadn't fired a single shot. Of 214 ambushed fights, in 144 (67%) neither of you had pulled a trigger before the first bullet landed on you — you didn't know the fight existed. And once it starts that way it takes a median 6.4 seconds to shoot back, by which time the exchange is usually decided. Those fights win at 6.2%.

The critical distinction: shooting first and hitting first are different things, and only the second one predicts anything. In wins you fired before being hit 43% of the time; in losses, 45%. The gap is entirely in who connects first — which is a question of who had their sights already on the right place, not who reacted faster.

This is the same thing PUBG guides call peeker's advantage and crosshair placement — pre-aiming where you expect a body to appear so that stepping out needs a small correction, not a full sweep. The data can't see your crosshair, but it can see that the team who connects first wins four and a half times more often.

Is this just "we lost too fast for the second player to shoot"?

That was the obvious objection, so I tested it. Restricting to fights that lasted more than 30 seconds — plenty of time for both to join — fighting together wins 20.0% and fighting alone wins 6.0%. Restricting instead to fights where both of you were alive and on your feet when it started, and lasting 15s+: 23.2% versus 8.2%. The effect survives every control I could put on it. It isn't an artifact of losing quickly; it's a cause of losing.

Speed of joining matters more than joining at all. When the second player starts shooting within 2 seconds, you win 47.8% — nearly triple your baseline. Between 2 and 15 seconds it falls to 15–20%. Past 15 seconds it recovers slightly (23.9%), but those are a different animal: long-range fights where there was never a shared burst of pressure. The winning pattern is both guns arriving essentially at once.

There's also an asymmetry in who ends up alone. S821 fights alone 82 times and wins 24.4% of those; D282 fights alone 66 times and wins 10.6%. Being caught solo hurts both, but it is roughly twice as costly for D282.

05

You Fight Too Hurt, Too Soon

This one didn't show up in the first version of this analysis at all, and it may be the cheapest thing on the page to fix.

Fight win rate by the lower of your two health values when the shooting started. The gradient is clean and steep.

Below 50 health, you win 4.2% of fights. At full health you win 25.6%. That's a six-fold difference, and it's monotonic all the way down: 100hp → 25.6%, 75–99 → 17.7%, 50–75 → 13.3%, under 50 → 4.2%. And 96 of your 365 fights — better than one in four — start with one of you already under half health.

Where the damaged fights come from

Almost entirely from re-engaging too soon after the last fight. Sorting every fight by how long since the previous one in the same match:

A fight taken within two minutes of the previous one starts at a median of 29 health and wins 7.1% of the time. The pattern is: fight, survive, don't reset, get caught again while still broken.

It's also the strongest predictor of being ambushed. In fights where the enemy hit you first, one of you was already below 75 health 48% of the time. In fights where you hit first, only 15%. Being hurt and being surprised are the same failure — you're moving, or healing in the open, or pushing to finish something, instead of being behind cover with your sights up.

What To Focus On

Ordered by expected gain. The first three are worth more than everything else on this page combined, because they're the three factors that actually separated your wins from your losses.

Biggest gain · n=365

1. Never take a second fight without resetting first

Fights starting under 50 health win 4.2%; at full health, 25.6%. A quarter of your fights start below half health, and fights taken within two minutes of the last one start at a median of 29 health and win 7.1%.

Do: after any fight, the next action is not looting and not pushing — it's break line of sight, get behind hard cover, heal and boost to full. Guides put this the same way: pre-heal and pre-boost before you push, not during, so you enter at 100 with a full bar. If you can't safely reset, you can't safely fight — leave instead.

Biggest gain · n=138 vs 227

2. Both guns, or no fight

Only one of you damages the enemy in 62% of fights, and those win 11.9% vs 26.1% when both engage. Joining within 2 seconds wins 47.8%. The effect survives every control — it isn't just "we died before he could shoot".

Do: make it an explicit rule that nobody opens unless both of you have an angle. Call the target, count down, fire together. If only one of you can see it, the answer is to reposition until both can — or to leave it alone. A solo opening is a fight you've already half-lost.

Biggest gain · n=214 ambushes

3. Stop being the one who gets seen first

You're hit first in 59% of fights and win those 7.0%. In two-thirds of them you hadn't fired at all — you didn't know the fight existed — and it takes 6.4 seconds to shoot back.

Do: two habits. Crosshair up and pre-aimed at head height on the angle you're about to clear, so first contact needs a small correction rather than a sweep. And stop moving before you look — most of your ambushes happen while relocating. Clear with a shoulder-peek from cover rather than walking into the open and scanning from there.

Strong · n=73 vs 81

4. Stop pushing; let them come

When you close the distance you win 11.0%. When they close on you, 21.0%. In fights you won, the enemy had moved 38m toward you in the previous 30 seconds; in losses, 20m.

Do: when you hear or spot a team that hasn't seen you, the default should be hold and set a crossfire, not close the gap. Pushing hands them the first-hit advantage, which is the single biggest predictor of losing. Push only when they're already broken or you have a clear flank.

Strong · n=31 vehicle fights

5. Park early and finish on foot

Fights that start with you in a vehicle win 9.7% with a −112 damage differential — your worst starting condition. You're three times more likely to be ambushed in a car than in fights you initiate.

Do: stop 200–300m short of where you're actually going and walk the last stretch. The car announces you to everyone in the zone; arriving quietly is most of what "rotate early" is actually buying you.

Solid · n=31 vs 52

6. Take the high side

More than 5m above the enemy: 35.5% win rate. Level: 16.3%. More than 5m below: 11.5%. You only have the height edge in 8% of your fights.

Do: when choosing between two positions that both cover the angle, take the higher one, and avoid fighting uphill into a compound. Being below someone is close to a risk factor in its own right.

Solid · n=45

7. D282: change the gun and the distance

M249 (32 fights, 15.6%) and AK-M (14 fights, 0 wins) are 46 of his fights at ~20–25m. Mini 14 (33.3%) and Mk12 SPR (21.4%) are his best, both used near 55m. He also fights 7m closer than S821 on average and wins 4.4% when ambushed against S821's 17.9%.

Do: D282 runs a DMR plus one AR and deliberately holds the longer angle, leaving the close side to S821. That plays to his best numbers and removes him from the sub-30m ambushes he recovers from worst.

Context · n=110 early fights

8. The early game is where all of this compounds

110 of your 365 fights happen in the first five minutes, 75% of them inside 30m, 65% inside compounds, with only 34% having both of you shooting. That's every risk factor at once.

Do: this is mostly fixed by the drop choices in the Debrief — land where you can gear up uncontested, and treat the first five minutes as looting time, not fighting time. Every early fight you decline is a fight you were probably going to lose.

If you only change one thing: make "reset to full health before the next fight" a hard rule, and make "we both shoot or nobody shoots" the second one. Those two conditions alone separate a 26% win rate from a 12% one, and unlike aim or reflexes they're pure discipline — you can do them perfectly from the very next game.

Method & Caveats

  • Source: raw PUBG telemetry for 213 matches (2 Jun – 25 Aug 2026) — position pings every 10s per player, plus every damage, knock, kill and shot event.
  • A fight = a cluster of combat events involving either player, split whenever 45s pass with no combat. 564 total; 20 bot-only fights are excluded everywhere (you win 60% of those and they'd flatter the numbers); 179 ended with nobody downed and are excluded from win rates. That leaves 365 decisive fights.
  • Win = you knocked or killed someone and neither of you went down. Trade = both sides lost someone. Loss = only your side.
  • "Hit first" means landed the first damaging hit. PUBG doesn't log misses, so this is about who connects, not who pulls the trigger — the two are tracked separately and only connecting predicts the outcome.
  • Approach is computed by comparing each side's position 30s before contact with their position at contact, so "they came to us" means they genuinely closed ground.
  • Health is from the position ping nearest the start of the fight (within 15s), so it's accurate to roughly a ten-second window, not the exact instant.
  • Terrain uses PUBG's named-location tag; unnamed farm buildings count as "open".
  • Confounds: the "both engaged" result was re-tested restricting to long fights and to fights where both started alive, and it held. Most other findings are simple correlations across your own games — they're strong enough to act on, but they aren't controlled experiments.
  • Small samples are labelled inline wherever they're under about n=20. The 80–200m bracket, the zero-risk-factor bucket and several weapon rows are in that category.
