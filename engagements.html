The Engagement Report

Every fight from 268 matches rebuilt from telemetry and replayed shot by shot — who fired, at whom, from how far, and how many seconds passed before the second of you joined in. Fewer averages, more actual fights. This read also has a real answer to the question the last one could only guess at.

D282 & S821 · Duo FPP · 2 Jun – 11 Sep 2026

This version is built around the fights themselves rather than around averages. Every combat event is grouped into a discrete engagement, reconstructed second by second, and sorted into the situation it belongs to. You can read any individual fight in Chapter 7.

Doing it that way also caught a mistake in the previous version, so that comes first.

00

A Correction

Three findings from the last version don't survive a closer look. Better to say so plainly than to leave you training against noise.

The health finding was mostly a measurement artifact. I was reading each player's health from the position ping nearest the start of the fight — and pings land every 10 seconds, so that "starting" health was often actually measured a few seconds into the fight. Losers get shot, so their health looked low before the fight had even begun. Measuring strictly from the last ping before first contact, the gap shrinks from +32 points to +13.6, and the "you win 4.2% below 50 health" claim disappears — only 32 of 392 fights actually start below 75 health, not the quarter I reported.

Also dropped

"Stop pushing; let them come." On 229 matches this is dead flat — you win 18.1% of fights whether you closed the distance or not (52.1% of wins involved a push, versus 51.2% of losses). The earlier 11% vs 21% split did not hold up with more data.

"Vehicles are your worst starting state." Also flat as a general claim (+1.9). There is a real and narrower version of it — being ambushed while mounted is genuinely bad — and that survives as one of the situations below.

"Multi-team fights are worse." Flat too (+0.8). They're common, not especially lethal.

What's left after that pruning is a much shorter list, but it's a list you can trust: two things dominate, one matters moderately, and the rest is noise.

In 67% of ambushes, neither of you had fired a shot. 179 of the 267 fights where the enemy hit first began with both of you completely unaware — no shots fired, no contact made. Those win 6.7%. This isn't a gunfight you're losing; it's a gunfight you didn't know had started until you were already down 40 health. Both headline numbers (33.5% / 7.9%) moved less than a point from the last read on 84 more fights — this finding is as settled as anything on this page gets.

Worth being precise: PUBG doesn't log misses, so this measures who connects first, not who pulls the trigger first. Whether you fired before being hit turns out not to predict anything at all — it's landing the first hit that matters, which is a question of already having your sights on the right place.

It isn't just "we died before he could shoot"

That's the obvious objection so it's worth killing off: restricting to fights that lasted more than 30 seconds — plenty of time for both of you to join — fighting together still wins 22.2% against 8.7% alone. The effect survives the control, essentially unchanged from the last read (21.9% / 6.8%).

Timing matters more than participation. When the second gun arrives inside 2 seconds you win 44.4% (down a little from 50% on 3 more fights in that bucket, still comfortably the best category here). Between 2 and 15 seconds it's still 12–20%. The winning pattern is unchanged: both of you opening essentially at once is what "both ready before we engage" actually buys you.

Sometimes nothing measurable separates them. The close-range pair is still the starkest example — 10 metres, 38 metres apart, both at full health, one player leading — and one ends 200-to-0 and the other 61-to-200. The new knife-range pair (added on this refresh) makes the same point at even closer range: 17 metres, near-full health on both sides, and one side walks away having dealt more than it took while the other doesn't. Whatever decided those fights (who saw whom, who was already aiming at the door, who hit their shots) isn't in the telemetry.

That's the honest ceiling on this kind of analysis, and it's why the useful findings are the big structural ones — which fight you take — rather than fine-grained tuning. You can't out-analyse a coin flip at 10 metres; you can avoid being at 10 metres without the first shot.

Four sessions in, not one

30 Aug (27 fights, 29.6% win rate), 31 Aug (19, 21.1%), 5 Sep (33, 27.3%), 11 Sep (32, 21.9%) — every single one of the four sessions since the advice landed above the 17.3% baseline that came before it. That's the difference between "one good night" and a real, repeatable shift: 111 fights is more than a quarter of the whole post-advice dataset, spread across a full two weeks, and it hasn't reverted once.

It worked — and the mechanism is now confirmed, not guessed at. The "both fired" number is still down (30.6%, from 37.8%), so this was never about more shared damage. What's up, consistently, across all four sessions, is how often you land the first hit (52.3% vs 41.4% baseline). Being ready together makes you spot people first, and increasingly one of you finishes it alone before the second gun is even needed. The preparation shows up as first contact, not as shared damage — exactly the read from the last version, now backed by four sessions instead of one.

Still worth a note of caution on "full HP at contact" (64.9%→59.5%, down) and "started in vehicle" (9.9%→7.2%, down) — neither is the mechanism, and both moved slightly the "wrong" way without hurting the win rate, which is a useful reminder that not every number needs to move for the important one to.

What To Focus On

Short list, because only a short list survived. Everything here is either the dominant effect or a specific situation with a specific answer.

Dominant · n=476

1. Win the first hit, or don't take the fight

You hit first: 33.5%. They hit first: 7.9%. In 67% of ambushes neither of you had fired at all, and it still takes 6.5 seconds to shoot back — identical to the last read. Every situation in the table is downstream of this one number, and it's barely moved on 84 more fights.

Do: pre-aim the angle you're about to clear at head height so first contact needs a small correction, not a sweep. And treat "taking fire from somewhere unknown" as an automatic break-contact call — the 7.9% is what happens when you try to fight it out instead.

Second largest · n=172 vs 304 · now confirmed over 4 sessions

2. Keep doing whatever you started on 30 August

Both firing wins 26.7% vs 14.8%, and it holds when you restrict to long fights (22.2% vs 8.7%). Second gun inside 2 seconds still wins the most of any bucket on the page (44.4%). Since the advice landed, first-hit rate is up to 52.3% and win rate to 25.2% — and unlike last read, that's now four sessions and 111 fights confirming it, not one.

Do: keep the "both set before we open" habit — the data still says its real payoff is spotting them first, not the second gun. Call the target, confirm you both have the angle, then open together.

Specific · n=21, zero wins

3. Indoors and taking fire = leave, not fight

Ambushed inside a building: 0 wins in 21 attempts now (was 17), average damage deficit 156. Four more of these landed in the last 39 games and all four went the same way.

Do: if the first shot comes while you're in a building you didn't clear, get out of the doorway sightline immediately rather than trading. There is still no example in 268 matches of that fight going your way.

Specific · n=23, unchanged since last read

4. Dismount before the last stretch

Being ambushed while mounted wins 8.7% with a −109 damage deficit — the exact same 23 fights as the last read; not one new instance in 39 games. (Driving in general is fine, and may even be a slight positive now — it's specifically being caught mounted that hurts.)

Do: stop 200–300m short of where you're actually going and walk in. You give up nothing but the noise that told everyone you were coming.

Moderate · n=54

5. Take the high side when it's free

More than 5m above the enemy: 37.0% win rate, against 18.1% on level ground and just 10.4% when you're the one at a height disadvantage. Cleaner and stronger than the last read's version of this stat, on more fights (54, up from 31) — and you only get it in about 11% of fights.

Do: when two positions both cover the angle, take the higher one. Not worth a detour; worth the choice.

If you only remember one line: your fights are decided before the shooting starts. 192 of 476 begin with them shooting first and those win under 15%; 122 begin with you shooting first and those win 37–57%. Everything worth practising is about which of those two you're in — and that split has only gotten sharper with more data, not softer.

Method & Caveats

  • Source: raw PUBG telemetry, 268 matches, 2 Jun – 11 Sep 2026. Position pings every 10s per player plus every damage, knock, kill and shot event.
  • A fight = combat events involving either player, split whenever 45s pass with no combat. 720 total; 20 bot-only fights excluded; 224 ended with nobody downed and are excluded from win rates. 476 decisive fights remain (91 wins, 145 trades, 240 losses; baseline win rate 19.1%, up from 18.1% at the last read).
  • Health is the last position ping strictly before first contact. The previous version used the nearest ping either side, which contaminated it with damage taken during the fight — see Chapter 00.
  • "Hit first" = landed the first damaging hit. Misses aren't logged, so this is who connects, not who fires.
  • Situations are assigned by priority, most-decisive condition first, so each fight appears in exactly one row.
  • Small samples are shown inline. Anything under about n=20 (fighting a man down) is indicative, not settled. The post-advice comparison in Chapter 06 (n=111 across four sessions) has grown past that threshold since the last read.
  • These are correlations from your own games, not experiments. The "both fired" result is the only one I could control properly; it held.
