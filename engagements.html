Doing it that way also caught a mistake in the previous version, so that comes first.

00

A Correction

Three findings from the last version don't survive a closer look. Better to say so plainly than to leave you training against noise.

The health finding was mostly a measurement artifact. I was reading each player's health from the position ping nearest the start of the fight — and pings land every 10 seconds, so that "starting" health was often actually measured a few seconds into the fight. Losers get shot, so their health looked low before the fight had even begun. Measuring strictly from the last ping before first contact, the gap shrinks from +32 points to +13.6, and the "you win 4.2% below 50 health" claim disappears — only 32 of 392 fights actually start below 75 health, not the quarter I reported.

Also dropped

"Stop pushing; let them come." On 229 matches this is dead flat — you win 18.1% of fights whether you closed the distance or not (52.1% of wins involved a push, versus 51.2% of losses). The earlier 11% vs 21% split did not hold up with more data.

"Vehicles are your worst starting state." Also flat as a general claim (+1.9). There is a real and narrower version of it — being ambushed while mounted is genuinely bad — and that survives as one of the situations below.

"Multi-team fights are worse." Flat too (+0.8). They're common, not especially lethal.

What's left after that pruning is a much shorter list, but it's a list you can trust: two things dominate, one matters moderately, and the rest is noise.

In 68% of ambushes, neither of you had fired a shot. 155 of the 228 fights where the enemy hit first began with both of you completely unaware — no shots fired, no contact made. Those win 7.1%. This isn't a gunfight you're losing; it's a gunfight you didn't know had started until you were already down 40 health.

Worth being precise: PUBG doesn't log misses, so this measures who connects first, not who pulls the trigger first. Whether you fired before being hit turns out not to predict anything at all — it's landing the first hit that matters, which is a question of already having your sights on the right place.

It isn't just "we died before he could shoot"

That's the obvious objection so it's worth killing off: restricting to fights that lasted more than 30 seconds — plenty of time for both of you to join — fighting together still wins 21.9% against 6.8% alone. The effect survives the control.

Timing matters more than participation. When the second gun arrives inside 2 seconds you win 50%. Between 2 and 15 seconds it falls to 15–19%. The winning pattern is both of you opening essentially at once — which is what "both ready before we engage" actually buys you.

Sometimes nothing measurable separates them. In that last pair the setups are almost identical — 10 metres, 38 metres apart, both at full health, one player leading — and one ends 200-to-0 and the other 61-to-200. Whatever decided those two fights (who saw whom, who was already aiming at the door, who hit their shots) isn't in the telemetry.

That's the honest ceiling on this kind of analysis, and it's why the useful findings are the big structural ones — which fight you take — rather than fine-grained tuning. You can't out-analyse a coin flip at 10 metres; you can avoid being at 10 metres without the first shot.

It worked — but not through the mechanism you'd expect. The 30 Aug session is your best win rate of any sizeable session in the dataset. But the "both fired" number went down, not up. What went up is how often you landed the first hit (48.1%, well above your 41.4% average). Being ready together seems to have made you spot people first — and then one of you often finished it alone before the second gun was needed. The preparation showed up as first contact, not as shared damage.

Treat this as encouraging rather than proven: one session of 27 fights, and single sessions in this dataset have swung between 0% and 57% before on similar samples. Two or three more sessions would settle it. The thing to keep doing is whatever produced the first-hit improvement.

What To Focus On

Short list, because only a short list survived. Everything here is either the dominant effect or a specific situation with a specific answer.

Dominant · n=392

1. Win the first hit, or don't take the fight

You hit first: 32.9%. They hit first: 7.5%. In 68% of ambushes neither of you had fired at all, and it takes 6.5 seconds to shoot back. Every situation in the table is downstream of this one number.

Do: pre-aim the angle you're about to clear at head height so first contact needs a small correction, not a sweep. And treat "taking fire from somewhere unknown" as an automatic break-contact call — the 7.5% is what happens when you try to fight it out instead.

Second largest · n=145 vs 247

2. Keep doing whatever you did on 30 August

Both firing wins 27.6% vs 12.6%, and it holds when you restrict to long fights (21.9% vs 6.8%). Second gun inside 2 seconds wins 50%. Your last session's first-hit rate jumped to 48.1% and the win rate to 29.6%.

Do: keep the "both set before we open" habit — the data says its real payoff is spotting them first, not the second gun. Call the target, confirm you both have the angle, then open together.

Specific · n=17, zero wins

3. Indoors and taking fire = leave, not fight

Ambushed inside a building: 0 wins in 17 attempts, average damage deficit 154. The pattern in the walkthrough is standard — one enemy close, one outside covering the door.

Do: if the first shot comes while you're in a building you didn't clear, get out of the doorway sightline immediately rather than trading. There is no example in 229 matches of that fight going your way.

Specific · n=23

4. Dismount before the last stretch

Being ambushed while mounted wins 8.7% with a −109 damage deficit. (Driving in general is fine — it's specifically being caught in the vehicle that hurts.)

Do: stop 200–300m short of where you're actually going and walk in. You give up nothing but the noise that told everyone you were coming.

Moderate · n=31 vs 52

5. Take the high side when it's free

More than 5m above the enemy: wins are 16.9% of the time versus 7.9% of losses — a real but moderate edge, and you only have it in about 8% of fights.

Do: when two positions both cover the angle, take the higher one. Not worth a detour; worth the choice.

If you only remember one line: your fights are decided before the shooting starts. 160 of 392 begin with them shooting first and those win under 13%; 95 begin with you shooting first and those win 33–60%. Everything worth practising is about which of those two you're in.

Method & Caveats

  • Source: raw PUBG telemetry, 229 matches, 2 Jun – 30 Aug 2026. Position pings every 10s per player plus every damage, knock, kill and shot event.
  • A fight = combat events involving either player, split whenever 45s pass with no combat. 598 total; 20 bot-only fights excluded; 186 ended with nobody downed and are excluded from win rates. 392 decisive fights remain (71 wins, 118 trades, 203 losses; baseline win rate 18.1%).
  • Health is the last position ping strictly before first contact. The previous version used the nearest ping either side, which contaminated it with damage taken during the fight — see Chapter 00.
  • "Hit first" = landed the first damaging hit. Misses aren't logged, so this is who connects, not who fires.
  • Situations are assigned by priority, most-decisive condition first, so each fight appears in exactly one row.
  • Small samples are shown inline. Anything under about n=20 (long-range openers, man-down, repositioning, the 30 Aug session) is indicative, not settled.
  • These are correlations from your own games, not experiments. The "both fired" result is the only one I could control properly; it held.
