476 FIGHTS · WHICH GUN ACTUALLY WINS

The Armory

The Weapon Stats page ranks guns by accuracy and damage. This page asks a harder, more useful question: when a weapon was doing the work in a real fight, how often did that fight end in a win? One main (AR or LMG) and one long gun (DMR or SR) each — here's what the data says you should actually be carrying.

D282 & S821 · Duo FPP · built from 476 decisive engagements, 268 matches

Every gun on this site so far has been scored the same way: shots, hits, accuracy, damage per minute. Those numbers say how well you shoot a weapon. They don't say whether carrying it makes you more likely to win the fight it's in — a gun can look great on a range-test stat line and still be the reason you keep losing 1-on-1s, if it's mostly used in situations that were already bad news.

This page uses the Engagement Report's 476 reconstructed fights instead. Every one of them already has a "primary weapon" attached — whichever gun did most of a player's damage in that specific fight — so we can ask, weapon by weapon: when this gun led the fight, what fraction of the time did you actually win it? That's a different, and for choosing a loadout, more useful number than accuracy.

00

The Method, In Short

Three things worth knowing before any of the numbers below mean anything.

What "win rate" means here

Every fight gets a "primary weapon": whichever gun a player fired most often in that specific fight. The fight then counts as a win, trade (both sides took a knock/kill) or loss for that weapon. A weapon's win% is wins ÷ all decisive fights it led — trades and losses both count against it, same convention as the Engagement Report.

The obvious confound: it's not a controlled experiment. A DMR mostly gets used at range, where you have time to see the enemy coming; an LMG mostly gets used up close, where ambushes happen. Some of the gap between weapons below is genuinely about the gun. Some of it is about the situations that gun tends to get pulled into. Every chapter below checks this by also splitting each weapon's fights into you opened it vs you got ambushed — if a weapon still looks bad within just the "you opened it" bucket, that's a real weapon problem, not a confound.

Sample sizes below run from n=5 to n=66. Read every percentage next to its n, not instead of it — that discipline is called out explicitly wherever a number is thin. Full method notes in Chapter 08.

For D282: AR 18.2% beats LMG 10.9%, and DMR (23.4%) beats both. For S821: AR 31.7% beats LMG 13.8%, and SR (42.9%) plus DMR (36.4%) both beat AR by a wide margin. This isn't just about carrying an LMG being unlucky. For him it isn't a story about missed openers at all — it really is the weapon.

"SMG" appears in the underlying tables (MP5K for both, plus a little Beryl M762/Tommy Gun) but isn't a valid main-weapon slot under your own rule (AR or LMG main, DMR or SR long), so it's shown for context only and left out of the recommendations below.

M249 collapses completely when D282 is ambushed. Split by initiation: when D282 opens the fight with the M249, he wins 27.8% of the time (18 fights) — not great, but workable. When he's ambushed with it in his hands, he has won zero of 21. That's not a small-sample fluke; 21 is one of the bigger buckets on this whole page. A 71-pound belt-fed gun that's supposed to be your suppression tool is instead your single worst ambush weapon.

HK416 over AK-M — not because it wins more, because it survives more

Overall win rate is a coin flip between them (12.5% vs 10.5%, both thin at n=19–24) and both open fights at almost the same rate (~46–47%). The split is the tell: ambushed with the AK-M, D282 has won zero of 10. Ambushed with the HK416, he's won 15.4% of 13 — still not good, but not a wall of zeroes either. Since you can't choose when you get ambushed, the gun that gives you any chance in that situation is the better default.

RPD is a clear no on the current evidence — 0% in 7 fights, and only opens the fight 14.3% of the time (worst initiation rate of anything on this page). Small n, but there's nothing in it pulling the other way either.

Mk12 SPR wins the opener, then stalls

D282 opens the fight with the Mk12 SPR an incredible 88.2% of the time — the highest initiation rate of any weapon either of you carries — and still only converts 20.0% of those openers into wins (3 of 15). The damage differential is positive (+20.3, his best of any weapon), so he's landing real hits first. The fight just isn't closing out: 8 of his 17 Mk12 SPR fights end in a trade. That reads like a gun that opens well but doesn't have the follow-up rate to finish before the enemy recovers.

Mini 14 does what the SPR promises. Same role, similar initiation rate (70.0%), and it actually converts — 35.0% overall and 50.0% within just the fights D282 opens himself. It's also the only weapon on this page where D282's win rate clears 30%.

D282 has never carried a real sniper rifle long enough to get a read on it. No Kar98k, M24, AWM or Win94 shows up with enough shots to make this page's n≥5 cutoff. That's not a finding against SRs — it's a genuine gap in the data. Worth a deliberate session or two actually testing one, especially given what S821's M24 does in the next chapter.

The workhorse isn't the weak link, it's just not the sharpest tool

ACE32 is not a bad gun for S821 — positive damage differential (+16.9), a healthy 56% initiation rate, and by far his most combat time (32.0 minutes, 50% more than his next-busiest gun) and most kills (38, career-high). It's the reliable default and there's no case for dropping it. But per fight, it converts at 24.0% — worse than HK416's 41.7% on nearly identical range and role, and nowhere close to the small-sample Beryl M762 flash.

Beryl M762 (80.0%, n=5) is the single most interesting number on this page and the most fragile. Five fights is not a loadout decision on its own — but a +109.2 damage differential on top of an 80% win rate is a big enough signal to deliberately pick it up more often and see if it holds at n=15 or 20, rather than writing it off as noise.

Both LMGs are S821's floor, exactly like D282's. RPD (11.1%, and it's his second most-used weapon on the whole site at 1,730 shots) and M249 (10.0%) are his two worst performers regardless of role or range. RPD's damage differential (−56.8) is nearly as bad as D282's M249. High volume, low conversion — heavy LMG usage for either of you has not been buying wins.

55.0% accuracy. 315 damage per combat-minute. 77.5 kills per combat-hour. All from the Weapon Stats page's own numbers, on a genuinely usable 100-shot sample — more than double the damage output of literally any other gun either of you owns, and it isn't close. In-fight, the M24 wins 36.8% at a real, honest sniper range (87.2m median) and opens the fight almost three-quarters of the time (73.7%). Mini 14 might actually be better — on a much smaller sample

Nine fights isn't enough to overturn the M24 pick, but 55.6% win rate and a +56.5 damage differential, used at an even longer median range (112.5m) than the M24, is not a number to ignore either. Reasonable read: M24 is the proven, high-volume choice; Mini 14 is a very promising second option worth deliberately getting more reps on rather than defaulting away from whenever it drops.

SLR is the solid, unglamorous third option — positive damage differential, decent win rate, plenty of games to be confident in it (n=17). Mk12 SPR is S821's worst long gun by a wide margin (10.0%, −34.0 diff) despite a 60% initiation rate — the same "wins the opener, loses the fight" pattern D282 has with the identical weapon (see next chapter).

The HK416 gap is the loudest signal on this whole page

Same gun, similar range (19–20m), nearly identical initiation rate (~42–46%) — and S821 wins the fight more than three times as often (41.7% vs 12.5%). Range and situation are controlled for here about as well as this dataset allows; what's left is positioning, target prioritization, or just raw gunskill with this specific weapon. If only one HK416 drops, it should go to S821, not by default to whoever calls it first.

Mini 14 goes the same direction (S821 ahead, 55.6% vs 35.0%) but both of you clear 30%+ — it's a gun that rewards whoever's holding it, just more so for S821. Mk12 SPR flips: D282's the better pick there (17.6% vs 10.0%), for the same reason detailed in Chapter 03 — S821 doesn't open with it as reliably (60.0% vs D282's 88.2%) and both of you show the "wins the opener, loses the fight" pattern with it, S821 just more severely.

Meanwhile M249, RPD and MP5K don't meaningfully change hands — whoever's carrying them, the outcome sits in the same mediocre-to-bad range. That's the cleanest evidence that these three specifically are weapon problems, not player problems.

08

Method & Caveats

  • Source: the 476 decisive engagements from The Engagement Report (built from raw telemetry, 268 matches, 2 Jun – 11 Sep 2026), cross-referenced with the per-weapon combat-time stats behind Weapon Stats.
  • "Primary weapon" = the gun a player fired most often within one specific fight. A fight's outcome (win/trade/loss) is attributed to whichever player dealt the most damage in it, and to that player's primary weapon. This means a weapon gets credit or blame for fights it merely led, not necessarily fights where it landed the killing blow.
  • Category roll-ups (Chapter 01) pool every fight led by any weapon in that category (e.g. all AR fights together) for a larger, more stable sample than any single gun gets on its own.
  • The initiation split (we-opened-it vs ambushed) is the main confound control used throughout — see Chapter 00. It isn't a perfect control: multi-team fights, enemy skill, and plain variance in a 5–39-fight sample all still move these numbers around.
  • Small samples are named inline. Anything under n=10 (Beryl M762, MP5K for D282, both DMRs at the very low end) is a genuine finding worth acting on cautiously, not a settled one. The Beryl M762 and Mini 14-for-S821 results in particular are exciting but fragile — treat them as "test more," not "switch now."
  • Individual output stats (accuracy, damage/combat-minute, kills/combat-hour, cited for the M24 and elsewhere) come from weapon_stats.json and include a handful of matches where only one of you was in the squad; the engagement win-rate figures are always duo fights, sourced directly from the Engagement Report's 476.
  • These are correlations from your own games, same as the Engagement Report — not a controlled test. Treat this page as a strong prior for what to carry, not a guarantee.
