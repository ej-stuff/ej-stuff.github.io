511 FIGHTS · WHICH GUN ACTUALLY WINS

The Armory

The Weapon Stats page ranks guns by accuracy and damage. This page asks a harder, more useful question: when a weapon was doing the work in a real fight, how often did that fight end in a win? One main (AR or LMG) and one long gun (DMR or SR) each — plus a full look at every SMG either of you has picked up — here's what the data says you should actually be carrying.

D282 & S821 · Duo FPP · built from 511 decisive engagements, 284 matches

Every gun on this site so far has been scored the same way: shots, hits, accuracy, damage per minute. Those numbers say how well you shoot a weapon. They don't say whether carrying it makes you more likely to win the fight it's in — a gun can look great on a range-test stat line and still be the reason you keep losing 1-on-1s, if it's mostly used in situations that were already bad news.

This page uses the Engagement Report's 511 reconstructed fights instead. Every one of them already has a "primary weapon" attached — whichever gun did most of a player's damage in that specific fight — so we can ask, weapon by weapon: when this gun led the fight, what fraction of the time did you actually win it? That's a different, and for choosing a loadout, more useful number than accuracy.

00

The Method, In Short

Three things worth knowing before any of the numbers below mean anything.

What "win rate" means here

Every fight gets a "primary weapon": whichever gun a player fired most often in that specific fight. The fight then counts as a win, trade (both sides took a knock/kill) or loss for that weapon. A weapon's win% is wins ÷ all decisive fights it led — trades and losses both count against it, same convention as the Engagement Report.

The obvious confound: it's not a controlled experiment. A DMR mostly gets used at range, where you have time to see the enemy coming; an LMG mostly gets used up close, where ambushes happen. Some of the gap between weapons below is genuinely about the gun. Some of it is about the situations that gun tends to get pulled into. Every chapter below checks this by also splitting each weapon's fights into you opened it vs you got ambushed — if a weapon still looks bad within just the "you opened it" bucket, that's a real weapon problem, not a confound.

Sample sizes below mostly run from n=5 to n=80, with one deliberate exception: Chapter 06's SMG breakdown goes down to n=1 to show every gun either of you has tried, clearly flagged as single data points, not findings. Read every percentage next to its n, not instead of it. Full method notes in Chapter 09.

AK-M has fallen off a cliff. Last read this was a near-coin-flip with HK416 (10.5% on n=19). HK416 is the pick — not the highest raw win rate, but the only one that survives an ambush

M249 technically edges HK416 on overall win rate (12.8% vs 12.5%), but that's before you split by who opened the fight. Ambushed with the M249: zero of 21. Ambushed with the AK-M: zero of 20 (worse than last read's zero of 10 — still zero, now on double the sample). Ambushed with the HK416: 15.4% of 13 — still not good, but not a wall of zeroes. Since you can't choose when you get ambushed, the gun that gives you any chance in that situation is the better default, even though its headline win rate isn't the highest of the three.

RPD remains a clear no — still 0% in 7 fights, and still the worst initiation rate of anything on this page (14.3%).

Mk12 SPR wins the opener, then stalls

D282 opens the fight with the Mk12 SPR 83.3% of the time (was 88.2% on one fewer fight) — still the highest initiation rate of any weapon either of you carries — and still only converts 20.0% of those openers into wins (3 of 15). The damage differential is positive (+15.7, still one of his best), so he's landing real hits first. The fight just isn't closing out: 8 of his 18 Mk12 SPR fights end in a trade. That reads like a gun that opens well but doesn't have the follow-up rate to finish before the enemy recovers.

Mini 14 does what the SPR promises. Same role, similar initiation rate (70.0%), and it actually converts — 35.0% overall and 50.0% within just the fights D282 opens himself. It's still the only weapon on this page where D282's win rate clears 30%, and nothing changed for it this refresh — no new Mini 14 fights landed in the last 16 games.

New this refresh: a 6-fight look at the Dragunov, a semi-auto DMR. Opens every single fight (100%, small n) but converts at only 16.7% with a rough damage differential (−46.1). Too thin to act on either way — noted, not recommended.

D282 has still never carried a real bolt-action sniper rifle long enough to get a read on it. No Kar98k, M24, AWM or Win94 shows up with enough shots to make this page's n≥5 cutoff, even with 16 more games in the bank. That's not a finding against SRs — it's a genuine, persistent gap in the data. Worth a deliberate session or two actually testing one, especially given what S821's M24 does in the next chapter.

The workhorse isn't the weak link, it's just not the sharpest tool

ACE32 is not a bad gun for S821 — positive damage differential (+17.6), a healthy 51.7% initiation rate, and by far his most combat time (33.4 minutes, over 50% more than his next-busiest gun) and most kills (41, career-high). Beryl M762 (80.0%, n=5) is the single most interesting number on this page and the most fragile. Five fights is not a loadout decision on its own — but a +109.2 damage differential on top of an 80% win rate is a big enough signal to deliberately pick it up more often and see if it holds at n=15 or 20, rather than writing it off as noise.

Both LMGs are S821's floor, exactly like D282's. RPD (11.1%, and it's his second most-used weapon on the whole site at 1,730 shots) and M249 (10.0%) are his two worst performers regardless of role or range. RPD's damage differential (−56.8) is nearly as bad as D282's M249. High volume, low conversion — heavy LMG usage for either of you has not been buying wins.

All from the Weapon Stats page's own numbers, on a genuinely usable 110-shot sample — more than double the damage output of literally any other gun either of you owns, and it isn't close. In-fight, the M24's win rate has settled to 35.0% (was 36.8% on one fewer fight) but at an even longer real, honest sniper range than before (108.1m median, up from 87.2m) and it still opens the fight three-quarters of the time (75.0%). SLR has caught up to the M24 on win rate — but not on anything else

SLR's win rate jumped from 29.4% to 35.0% on three more fights (n=20 now) — exactly tying the M24. It also carries a positive damage differential (+17.3) where the M24's is negative (−22.5). On engagement win rate and damage differential alone, SLR now looks like the equal or better pick. But the M24's individual output (52.7% accuracy vs SLR's 18.1%, more than 100 more damage per combat-minute) is in an entirely different tier — the M24 is landing far more of its shots and doing far more per minute carried, it's just not converting that into as clean a damage differential per fight. Read together: M24 stays the pick, but SLR is now a genuinely strong second option, not just a fallback.

Mini 14 remains a real contender on a small sample — still 55.6% win rate and +56.5 damage differential on 9 fights, unchanged this refresh, at an even longer median range (112.5m) than the M24. Mk12 SPR is still S821's worst long gun by a wide margin (10.0%, −34.0 diff) despite a 60% initiation rate — the same "wins the opener, loses the fight" pattern D282 has with the identical weapon (see Chapter 07).

The HK416 gap is the loudest signal on this whole page

Same gun, similar range (19–20m), nearly identical initiation rate (~42–46%) — and S821 wins the fight more than three times as often (41.7% vs 12.5%). Range and situation are controlled for here about as well as this dataset allows; what's left is positioning, target prioritization, or just raw gunskill with this specific weapon. If only one HK416 drops, it should go to S821, not by default to whoever calls it first.

AK-M shows almost the same gap, and it's new this refresh. S821 wins 20.0% with it, D282 wins 6.2% — more than three times the difference, on real samples now (n=10 and n=32). D282's AK-M range and initiation rate (19.8m, 37.5%) aren't wildly different from S821's (10.3m, 70.0%), so this isn't purely a "S821 uses it more sensibly" story either. Between this and HK416, the pattern is now two-for-two: when an AR is in question, it's worth defaulting to S821 unless there's a specific reason not to.

Mini 14 goes the same direction (S821 ahead, 55.6% vs 35.0%) but both of you clear 30%+ — it's a gun that rewards whoever's holding it, just more so for S821. Mk12 SPR still flips: D282's the better pick there (16.7% vs 10.0%), for the same reason detailed in Chapter 03 — S821 doesn't open with it as reliably (60.0% vs D282's 83.3%) and both of you show the "wins the opener, loses the fight" pattern with it, S821 just more severely.

Meanwhile M249 and RPD don't meaningfully change hands — whoever's carrying them, the outcome sits in the same bad range for both of you. MP5K drifts a little toward S821 (21.1% vs 16.7%) but not by nearly as much as the ARs above — treat it as roughly weapon-neutral, same conclusion as Chapter 06's read on it.

Method & Caveats

  • Source: the 511 decisive engagements from The Engagement Report (built from raw telemetry, 284 matches, 2 Jun – 13 Sep 2026), cross-referenced with the per-weapon combat-time stats behind Weapon Stats.
  • "Primary weapon" = the gun a player fired most often within one specific fight. A fight's outcome (win/trade/loss) is attributed to whichever player dealt the most damage in it, and to that player's primary weapon. This means a weapon gets credit or blame for fights it merely led, not necessarily fights where it landed the killing blow.
  • Category roll-ups (Chapter 01) pool every fight led by any weapon in that category (e.g. all AR fights together) for a larger, more stable sample than any single gun gets on its own.
  • The initiation split (we-opened-it vs ambushed) is the main confound control used throughout — see Chapter 00. It isn't a perfect control: multi-team fights, enemy skill, and plain variance in a small sample all still move these numbers around.
  • Small samples are named inline. Anything under n=10 (Beryl M762, both Dragunov and Mk12 SPR at the low end, most of Chapter 06's SMGs) is a genuine finding worth acting on cautiously, not a settled one. The Beryl M762 and Mini 14-for-S821 results in particular are exciting but fragile — treat them as "test more," not "switch now." The same goes double for UMP45 in Chapter 06 (n=3–4 per player).
  • Individual output stats (accuracy, damage/combat-minute, kills/combat-hour, cited for the M24 and elsewhere) come from weapon_stats.json and include a handful of matches where only one of you was in the squad; the engagement win-rate figures are always duo fights, sourced directly from the Engagement Report's 511.
  • These are correlations from your own games, same as the Engagement Report — not a controlled test. Treat this page as a strong prior for what to carry, not a guarantee.
